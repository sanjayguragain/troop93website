Adventure. Service. Leadership.

A taste of life in Troop 93

From a first night under the stars to a challenging backcountry trek, our scouts learn by doing. Here are a few examples of the experiences that make up our troop's program.

camping

Camping & summer camp

Time outdoors builds independence, friendships, and practical skills, with patrols working together around camp.

Program highlights: Camp Morrison in Idaho, Camp Fiesta Island, and Lake Cachuma camping.

hiking

Hiking & backpacking

Scouts build confidence on beginner hikes and first backpacking trips, then work toward bigger mountain adventures.

Program highlights: Mount Baldy day hikes, High Sierra backpacking, and the John Muir Trail.

kayaking

Adventures on the water

Canoeing and rafting bring a different kind of teamwork, along with opportunities to learn swimming and water safety skills.

Program highlights: Colorado River canoeing, American River rafting, and the Swimming merit badge.

volunteer_activism

Service in our community

Giving back is part of scouting. Our scouts pitch in on projects that support neighbors and care for shared outdoor spaces.

Program highlights: Love Fullerton, Scouting for Food, native plant restoration, and trail work.

directions_bike

Cycling & outdoor challenges

There is always a new challenge to try, whether it is riding with friends, learning to climb, or tackling a ropes course.

Program highlights: holiday rides to the beach, rock climbing, and campouts with a ropes course.

explore

Skills, discovery & leadership

Hands-on activities help scouts explore interests, practice outdoor skills, and learn to lead their patrols.

Program highlights: map and compass, wilderness first aid, knot tying, 3D printing, and scout leadership training.

More than a destination

Scouts help make the adventure happen

At Troop 93, scouts help plan and run the program. The patrol method gives them a small team to work with, responsibilities to take on, and opportunities to learn from one another.

Adult leaders provide guidance while scouts practice making decisions. Planning a trip, preparing for the outdoors, and helping a newer scout all turn leadership into something they can do, rather than just talk about.

How our troop works →

  1. Bring an idea

    Scouts' interests help shape the troop program. Patrol leaders bring their patrols' ideas into the Patrol Leaders Council, with adult guidance.

  2. Build the skills

    Meeting activities such as navigation, first aid, and knot tying give scouts a chance to practice skills they can use outdoors.

  3. Take responsibility

    Outings put teamwork and preparation into practice. As scouts gain experience, they can take on leadership roles and help others learn.

A place to start. Room to grow.

A program for different experience levels

The troop's outdoor program includes first-year experiences as well as high adventure for older, more experienced scouts. Growth comes through practice, personal initiative, and taking on a little more responsibility along the way.

Start with the basics

First hikes, introductory backpacking, and advancement outings give new scouts a place to begin. Learning to prepare a pack and work with a patrol is part of the adventure.
