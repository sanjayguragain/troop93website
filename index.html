High adventure. Part of who we are.

High adventure is at the heart of Troop 93

High adventure is a central part of Troop 93's program. Our activity highlights include High Sierra backpacking, the John Muir Trail, Colorado River canoeing, and American River rafting.

Scouts start with hikes and introductory backpacking, building toward more challenging outings as their skills and experience grow. Along the way, they practice preparation, teamwork, and leadership. You don't have to aim for a backcountry trek to belong here. Camping, service, discovery, and leadership are equally valued parts of troop life.

The Orange County Council High Adventure Team (OCCHAT) offers trek ideas, adult leader training, and trail awards to help troops explore what's possible.

More than a destination

Scouts help make the adventure happen

At Troop 93, scouts help plan and run the program. The patrol method gives them a small team to work with, responsibilities to take on, and opportunities to learn from one another.

Adult leaders provide guidance while scouts practice making decisions. Planning a trip, preparing for the outdoors, and helping a newer scout all turn leadership into something they can do, rather than just talk about.

How our troop works →

  1. Bring an idea

    Scouts' interests help shape the troop program. Patrol leaders bring their patrols' ideas into the Patrol Leaders Council, with adult guidance.

  2. Build the skills

    Meeting activities such as navigation, first aid, and knot tying give scouts a chance to practice skills they can use outdoors.

  3. Take responsibility

    Outings put teamwork and preparation into practice. As scouts gain experience, they can take on leadership roles and help others learn.
