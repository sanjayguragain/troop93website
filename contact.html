For prospective scouts and families

Make the most of your first visit

Seeing a meeting is a useful way to understand a scout-led troop. You can meet the people, see how scouts work together, and ask the questions that matter to your family.

Before you come

Explore our activity examples and new-family questions to get familiar with the program. Think about what your scout would like to try and what you would like to learn.

Whether your scout is new to scouting, coming from a Cub Scout pack, or transferring from another troop, bring questions about the next step in their journey.

While you are here

Meet scouts and adult leaders and see how a troop meeting works. Ask about patrols, first-year activities, and how new scouts learn the skills to get outdoors.

Bring your questions about family involvement, equipment, costs, and fitting scouting around school and other commitments.

When you are ready

Talk with leaders about current registration requirements and next steps. Ask what your scout needs to get started before purchasing uniforms or camping equipment.

Our new-family questions cover common topics to help you prepare for that conversation.

Community connections

Community service and hands-on learning are part of troop life. Scouts have taken part in food drives, native plant restoration, and trail work. Families can learn about these experiences through our activity highlights.
